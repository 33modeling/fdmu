\begin{abstract}
Approximate LLM unlearning can preserve aggregate utility yet severely damage
a small set of retained behaviors. We ask whether such failures can
be anticipated before unlearning and selectively mitigated.

We introduce a sealed two-signal profile. \emph{Loss Susceptibility} is a
gradient-based measure: the squared norm of a retained behavior's loss gradient
restricted to a declared parameter block. A pool-shared forward-only estimator avoids
materializing candidate-specific gradients.
\emph{Representation Proximity} captures exposure to the forget request. Their
mixture is calibrated on target-request-disjoint development runs and fixed
before any target outcome is observed.

We test prospective damage prediction, estimator fidelity to its exact
direct-gradient target, and decision value for fixed-budget selective
protection.
\resph{Report the resolved prediction, fidelity, protection, and
failure-boundary results.} This connects pre-unlearning risk localization to
fixed-budget protection without modifying the parent method.

\end{abstract}
